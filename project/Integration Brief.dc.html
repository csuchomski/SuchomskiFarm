One farm, three apps, one shell

How I read the problem, what I still need from you, and the structure I'd propose before drawing any screens. Correct me anywhere — it's cheaper now than after four mockups.

What I understood
farm-app — inventory and the farm store. Owns products, stock, and the only surface a customer ever sees.
herd app — the animals. Identity, health, and lactation records: gallons per cow per lactation, and averages across the herd.
FarmFinanceTracker — the books. Sales revenue from the store, costs attributed to the herd, everything else.
Mixed livestock plus crops. Internal users are you and your bookkeeper; customers only touch the store.
The seam that matters

Milk is the spine. A cow produces gallons, gallons become store inventory, inventory sells, and the sale lands in the books. Costs run the other direction and land back on the cow.

cow→ lactation→ gallons→ inventory lot→ store order→ ledger entry

Cost and profit per head is the payoff of that chain, so it's the number I'd design the dashboard around.

Assumptions I'm making

Two shellsThe internal app and the customer store are one system but not one interface. Customers get a small, quiet storefront with none of the operational nav.
No modulesNav is by farm object — Herd, Store, Books, plus a Today home — not by which app the feature used to live in. A user should never need to know there were three apps.
Animal recordOne page per cow holding identity, health, the lactation curve, and her financial line — feed and vet cost in, milk revenue out. This is where the integration becomes visible.
BookkeeperSame shell, narrower permissions. She works in Books and needs sales to arrive from the store already categorized rather than re-entered.
MobilePhone use in the field is capture, not analysis: log a milking, log a treatment, check a cow. Desktop is for reading.
