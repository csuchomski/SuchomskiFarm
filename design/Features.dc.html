How it works

Four things on a farm. One set of records under them.

Most farms run a grazing chart, a herd book, a marketing hunch and an accountant's spreadsheet, and none of the four knows what the others did. Grazerbook is the four of them sharing one record — which is the only reason any of the numbers below can be worked out at all.

What's in it

Turn on what you run.

Grazing

The daily move, strip grazing by the wire, rest days, dry matter on offer, and a printable record filtered by round rather than by calendar month.

  • Paddocks and properties — owned, leased, shared
  • Several mobs, several pastures, at once
  • Rounds you start yourself
  • Forage balance and a grazing plan

Herd

Every head on the farm, what it cost, what it earned, who its dam was, and what it weighed the last three times you ran it over the scale.

  • Animals, with genetics and lifetime money
  • Milking and lactations
  • Breeding, sires and calvings
  • Alerts — withdrawal, due dates, what today needs

Market

Where the gain stops paying, and whether trading down the weight ladder beats keeping what you have — both read off real auction reports, not a guess.

  • USDA AMS summaries, priced at your weight
  • Value of gain against your cost of gain
  • Sell/buy across a whole draft, not one head
  • One class against another, over time

Books

A real ledger, not a category list — with the tax work that usually costs you a January weekend already done beside it.

  • Transactions, balance sheet, cash flow
  • Schedule F, line by line
  • Cattle depreciation
  • Costs attributed to the animals they were for

A farm store — products, orders, a delivery forecast and customers — is there too, for anyone selling beef, milk or eggs off the place.

What it won't do

Three things we'd rather say now than in a demo.

A farm screen full of confident numbers is easy to build and hard to trust. These are the places Grazerbook deliberately stops.

It won't tell you that you're compliant.

It keeps the record and prints it. Whether that record satisfies a programme is between you and the person administering it — no software can promise that, and the ones that imply it are selling you a risk.

It won't quote you a price for your cattle.

Auction summaries are what cattle actually sold for at that barn on that day. That is not a bid on yours, and every figure is gross — before commission, yardage, freight, shrink and death loss. The page says so, every time.

It won't fill a gap with a plausible number.

A week with no report is a break in the line, not a zero and not last week's figure carried forward. Sample data says it's a sample until you replace it.

Getting started

Your ground goes in once, and it's the last time you draw it.

01

Bring your paddocks

Import the fence lines you already have as KML from whatever mapped them, or draw them. Acres come out of the shapes.

02

Name your mobs

Yearlings, dry cows, the milking herd. Head counts and classes; animals individually if you keep them that way.

03

Move them tomorrow

That's the setup. Everything else — rounds, rest days, cost of gain, the tax schedule — builds itself out of the moves you were making anyway.
