01   The move

The job you do every morning, in about four taps.

Pick the mob, pick where they're going, set the wire. Grazerbook works out the strip, the days of rest on the paddock they're leaving, and the dry matter on offer — then logs it.

Built for a farm with real ground: forty-odd paddocks, several mobs, more than one property, and cattle that overwinter somewhere that isn't paddock one.

02   The round

A round is one mob through one pasture, once.

Not a calendar month, and not "whatever happened between the first and the thirty-first". Your grazing record opens on the round you are in, and you can start the next one yourself — because where a mob starts depends on where they overwintered and what you cut for hay, not on a paddock number.

03   The market

Cattle get cheaper as they get heavier. Most software never mentions it.

There is a weight past which another month of grass adds less value than it costs — while the animal goes on getting heavier and going on being worth more in total, which is what makes it so easy to miss.

Grazerbook reads USDA auction summaries, prices your class at the weight you actually have, and shows you where the margin peaks. Bud Williams' sell/buy, worked out rather than argued about.

04   The books

A cheque book that knows which cow it was for.

Transactions, balance sheet, cash flow, Schedule F and cattle depreciation — and any expense can be attributed to the animals it was actually for, so the vet bill on a heifer shows up on her record and not just in a category.

More than one business, kept apart. The farm and the rental have separate books and separate ground, and an entry filed against the wrong one takes about four seconds to move.
